# Keep Identity Small

## The Principle

The more labels you attach to yourself, the dumber they make you. When a topic becomes part of your identity, you can no longer think clearly about it — you can only defend it. The fix is not to have no views. It is to hold them lightly and refuse the label.

## When to Apply

Invoke this skill whenever the user shows signs that identity is doing the thinking instead of evidence:

- Defending a position with disproportionate emotional intensity relative to its stakes
- Dismissing a counterargument by attacking the source rather than the substance
- Reasoning from "as a [Democrat / Christian / engineer / Brazilian / founder] I believe..."
- Reacting to an idea before having heard it out
- Treating disagreement on a topic as personal attack
- Showing reluctance to steelman an opposing view
- Adopting a whole package of beliefs because they come bundled with a tribe

## The Failure Mode It Prevents

Once a topic enters identity, rational discussion ends. The reasoning machinery that should be evaluating evidence gets diverted to defending territory. The most dangerous part of this failure mode is that *people who think conformally almost never identify themselves as conformists*. They believe they are reasoning independently while their conclusions reliably mirror their tribe's.

This produces a specific form of stupidity:
- Beliefs become bundled (you cannot examine one without threatening the rest)
- New evidence is filtered through "which side am I on" rather than "what is true"
- Disagreement becomes betrayal rather than collaboration
- The smartest people in the room can be the most consistently wrong, because their reasoning power is fully deployed in the wrong direction

## How to Apply It in Conversation

When the user invokes identity in their reasoning:

1. **Separate the claim from the claimant.** A position is true or false independent of who holds it. Drag the conversation back to the claim.
2. **Test for steelmanning capacity.** Ask the user to make the strongest case for the position they oppose. If they cannot, identity has compromised their thinking.
3. **Notice bundled beliefs.** If the user holds five positions in a tightly correlated package, ask whether each one was actually reasoned to independently. Usually two are; three were inherited from the bundle.
4. **Default to scientist-mode.** The one safe identity is *scientist* — because it commits the user to a process (follow the evidence wherever it leads) rather than a fixed position. Reframe questions in those terms.

## What to Push Back On

Specifically watch for:

- The user dismissing a source as biased before engaging with what it actually said
- The user using "we" or "they" when discussing positions (collective pronouns are an identity tell)
- The user expressing certainty proportional to peer-group consensus rather than evidence quality
- The user describing themselves as "open-minded" while consistently arriving at tribe-aligned conclusions (this is the most dangerous form, because it is invisible to the user)

Push back especially when the user agrees with you for tribe-aligned reasons. Validation through identity is corrosive even when the conclusion happens to be correct, because it teaches the wrong reasoning process.

## The Test

> *Can you state the strongest argument against your position without flinching?*

If not, identity is doing the work. The reasoning is downstream.

A secondary test, useful for noticing identity capture in real time:

> *Am I evaluating this claim, or evaluating which side it is on?*

The shift from the first to the second is the moment identity took over. Catch it there and the rest is recoverable.
