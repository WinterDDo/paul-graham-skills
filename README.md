# Paul Graham, Distilled

Six principles compressed from twenty years of Paul Graham's essays. The goal is not to summarize him — that has been done. The goal is to find the smallest set of ideas that, if internalized, would change how a thinking person operates.

This is a reading. It is not a tool, a framework, or a Claude Code extension. It is the closest thing to a written answer to the question: *if you had ten minutes to transmit what matters most across PG's body of work, what would you say?*

Six is what survived the cut.

## The Six Principles

1. **Curiosity Is the Engine, Not the Fuel** — External motivators are always worse guides than intrinsic interest.
2. **Optimize for the Thing, Not the Metric** — The most dangerous form of waste is effort that *looks* like progress.
3. **Identity Makes You Stupid** — The more labels you claim, the dumber they make you.
4. **What You Work On Matters More Than How Hard You Work** — The choice of problem is the most important decision.
5. **An Idea Is Not Finished Until It Is Written** — Writing is not how you express thoughts; it is how you find out what you think.
6. **Good Work Compounds — Start Before You Are Ready** — Readiness is downstream of doing the work.

For the full treatment, see **[PRINCIPLES.md](PRINCIPLES.md)**.

## How These Were Distilled

Source: roughly 25 of PG's most thinking-oriented essays plus several auxiliary pieces (Founder Mode, How to Disagree, The Right Kind of Stubborn, Four Quadrants of Conformism, A Project of One's Own).

The distillation followed a strict three-domain test: a candidate principle had to apply meaningfully in at least three unrelated contexts (software, business strategy, personal life) to qualify. Principles that worked in only one domain were treated as instances rather than principles.

## Source

All ideas trace to essays by [Paul Graham](https://paulgraham.com/articles.html). The principles are his; the compression is the contribution.
